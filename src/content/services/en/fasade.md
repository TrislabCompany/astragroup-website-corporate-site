faq:
  title: "Common questions about facades"
  items:
    - question: "When is it time to renovate a facade?"
      answer: "When the render cracks or falls off, when stains, algae or mould appear, and when heating and cooling bills are high. An old facade without insulation is often the cause of both."
    - question: "Polystyrene or stone wool?"
      answer: "Polystyrene (EPS) is lighter and cheaper and insulates well. Stone wool doesn't burn, lets water vapour through better and dampens noise. For older walls and taller buildings stone wool is often the better choice; at the site visit we tell you what suits your house."
    - question: "How thick should the insulation be?"
      answer: "We choose the thickness by the wall you have and by how much you want to cut heat loss. When older houses are renovated it is usually thicker than it once was, because every extra centimetre shows on the heating bills."
    - question: "Which colour should I choose?"
      answer: "Lighter colours heat up less in the sun and stay good-looking for longer, which matters on the coast and in the Karst. Very dark colours are not advised on an insulated facade, because the surface gets too hot. Manufacturers state for each colour whether it is suitable."
    - question: "Can a facade be done in any weather?"
      answer: "No. Adhesives and renders need dry weather without frost, strong sun or wind, so we plan the work around the forecast and the season."

We put up new facades and renovate existing ones: classic and ventilated facades, and energy renovations that cut a house's heat loss.

A good facade protects the house from the weather and keeps it cooler in summer and warmer in winter, so heating and cooling bills go down. Every house is different, so we choose the facade together, after a site visit.
